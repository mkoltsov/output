Game design

HEXXAGO: The Resource Frontier

1. Purpose, scope and evidence

HEXXAGO is an original, peaceful strategy game about allocating space on a small shared frontier. Two equal teams survey a stylized archipelago, establish markers, and persuade adjacent settlements to join their network. A move can grow a network or reposition it. A successful conversion changes a marker's affiliation; it does not depict violence, an invasion or the annexation of a real country.

The core experience is an immediately readable abstract board game with the presentation quality of a modern strategy dashboard: tactile hexagonal land, a calm ocean, warm typography, visible resource ledgers and quick access to the next meaningful decision.

This document serves two purposes:

  1. Specify the rules and intended player experience in enough detail to implement and maintain them.
  2. Separate source-inspected implementation, design targets and future work. A feature described as a target is not thereby delivered or tested.

The implementation snapshot was inspected on 31 October 2026 UTC while the game was being assembled. The final documentation readback included the engine, board scene, frontend orchestration/styles, country registry, audio manager, assets and packaging configuration. Browser acceptance, desktop launch and publication are separate milestones; this document is not a receipt for any of them. See RELEASE.md for their boundaries.

The authoritative local rule implementation is src/game/rules.ts. Its current behavior is specified below, including deliberately bounded repetition and resource rules. Implementation and release status must be reconciled from the actual integrated build.

1.3 Original synthesis

HEXXAGO takes the clone/jump/conversion interaction as its abstract rules foundation and develops a distinct resource economy, equal-country identities, original island art, quiet synthesized sound and dashboard UX around it. It does not implement HEXSTORM's towers, combat waves, moving ship, story, metaprogression or energy-network destruction.

The commercial analogy is a polished HTML5 game that can also be distributed as a desktop application. It is not a promise to reproduce either reference's product, exact engine version, content scale, platform certification or audience.

2. Creative pillars

2.1 A readable decision every turn

A new player should understand three questions without opening a manual:

  • Which of my markers can move?
  • Where will cloning or jumping leave me?
  • Which neighboring markers would change affiliation?

Selection, legal destinations and contextual explanations must agree with the engine. Hover effects are supportive; the full interaction must work without hover.

2.2 Peaceful competition with recognizable identities

Real country names and small flag symbols make a match easy to personalize. They convey no resource multiplier, attack strength, intelligence level or cultural trait. Every country uses exactly the same legal moves, starting count, shield cost, income and victory rules.

The archipelago is fictional. There are no real borders, real-world territorial claims, current conflicts, historical conquest scenarios, population losses, weapons or national superiority ratings.

2.3 Territory first; resources create choices

Winning territory remains the primary objective. Resource mode adds a public ledger and one concrete ability: spend three ore to shield an owned tile before moving. Energy and food contribute to a territorial tie, not to undisclosed spells or planned mechanics.

Every UI description must match this small shipped ability set. There are no energy attacks, food recruitment bonuses, country ultimates, paid boosts or hidden research trees.

2.4 A complete small game

The first complete product has solo AI, local hotseat, two modes, two resource layouts, clear results, audio settings and local continuation. Online accounts, matchmaking, campaigns, procedural content and platform integrations are outside the initial critical path.

2.5 A tactile, calm strategy instrument

The ocean/ivory/forest/bronze palette, restrained movement and soft synthesized timbres should invite another thoughtful match. Clarity takes precedence over ornamental detail. Sound, animation and country color must never be the sole carrier of a rule.

3. Player promise and match structure

Working title: HEXXAGO: The Resource Frontier.

Genre: turn-based abstract strategy.

Players: one person versus a local computer opponent, or two people sharing one device.

Runtime: local browser logic; optional desktop shell uses the same game build. No gameplay server or model service is required.

Session expectation: a short skirmish rather than an open-ended campaign. The hard limit is 180 individual turns, including automatic passes. A specific duration in minutes is a playtest question, not an established product claim.

The intended loop is:

Home → Skirmish setup → First-turn briefing → Select a marker
   → Inspect clone/jump options → Optional ore shield → Move
   → Read conversions/income → Opponent turn → Repeat
   → Result explanation → Rematch, new setup or home

“Continue” restores the local match and its settings where supported. It is not cross-device synchronization or Steam Cloud.

4. Board geometry and maps

4.1 Coordinates and distance

The rules use axial coordinates (q, r), with implicit cube coordinate s = −q −r. A radius-four board contains all coordinates satisfying:

max(|q|, |r|, |q + r|) ≤ 4

The full hexagon contains:

1 + 3R(R + 1) = 1 + 3 × 4 × 5 = 61 cells

Three nonplayable coordinates are removed:

(1, 1), (−2, 1), (1, −2)

The result is exactly 58 playable cells. Holes do not contain markers, resources or legal destinations. They remain recognizable as absent land.

The movement distance between cells a and b is:

Δq = b.q − a.q
Δr = b.r − a.r
d(a,b) = max(|Δq|, |Δr|, |Δq + Δr|)
       = (|Δq| + |Δr| + |Δq + Δr|) / 2

The six adjacent directions are:

(1,0), (0,1), (−1,1), (−1,0), (0,−1), (1,−1)

4.2 Opening position

Player 0 owns:

(4,0), (−4,4), (0,−4)

Player 1 owns:

(0,4), (−4,0), (4,−4)

The six outer corners alternate ownership. Each side starts with three pieces, no resources and no shields. There are 52 empty playable cells. No starting corner is a resource deposit.

The current engine starts Player 0. Selecting a country does not change this. Alternating the starting seat in a rematch is a future fairness improvement unless frontend behavior explicitly implements it; it is not an engine capability to assume.

4.3 What symmetry does and does not prove

The removed cells are invariant under a 120° rotation:

R120(q,r) = (−q−r, q)

More importantly for two-player fairness, the reflection:

S(q,r) = (r,q)

preserves the playable field and exchanges the two starting teams. Every resource type is also placed in reflected pairs. This proves equal geometric access under a player-swapping transformation. It does not prove equal win rates, erase Player 0's initiative, or remove the second player's final opportunity to change ownership before round income.

Do not describe this three-hole board as having sixfold or 180° rotational symmetry.

4.4 Frontier

Frontier is the baseline Resource layout. Each listed base coordinate also creates its reflected partner (r,q):

  • Energy bases: (0,2), (−3,0), (−1,3).
  • Ore bases: (0,3), (−2,0), (−3,1).
  • Food bases: (−2,2), (−3,3), (−2,3).

This produces six energy, six ore and six food nodes: 18 resource cells and 40 plain cells. Resource density is 18/58, approximately 31.0%.

The distributed sites encourage more than a single central race. Outer deposits give openings a readable objective; moving away from a deposit creates a genuine opportunity cost because ownership follows the occupying marker.

4.5 Struggle

Struggle retains Frontier's complete field and adds these reflected pairs:

  • Energy: (1,0) and (0,1).
  • Ore: (−1,0) and (0,−1).
  • Food: (−1,1) and (1,−1).

This produces eight of each type: 24 resource cells and 34 plain cells. Resource density is 24/58, approximately 41.4%.

The six extra sites sit near the center. The intended effect is earlier resource contact and more opportunities for resource conversion, without changing movement rules or adding a new ability.

4.6 Classic map behavior

Classic mode sets every terrain cell to plain. Both map names therefore have the same current geometry and starting pieces in Classic. There are not two mechanically different Classic boards in this implementation. Setup copy must not advertise them as such.

Future genuinely different maps require explicit layouts, starting-access analysis and separate acceptance; a map label alone is not new content.

5. Exact movement and conversion rules

5.1 A legal move

A move identifies a source and a destination. It is legal only when:

  1. The match is still active.
  2. Both IDs identify playable cells.
  3. The source belongs to the active player.
  4. The destination is empty.
  5. The source-to-destination distance is one or two.

Neither resource balances nor country selection affect movement legality. A player cannot move onto an opponent's occupied cell directly.

5.2 Clone: distance one

The destination becomes owned by the moving player and the source remains occupied. This creates one new piece.

Example: moving from (0,0) to (1,0), when both cells exist and the latter is empty, retains the original marker and places a second one next to it.

5.3 Jump: distance two

The source is vacated and the destination becomes owned by the moving player. The number of occupied cells does not change before conversions.

A jump uses hex distance, not a pathfinding route. It can cross a hole or occupied intermediate position. There is no requirement for an empty middle cell. The destination itself must still be playable and empty.

Example: (0,0) to (2,0) is a jump. (0,0) to (2,−1) is also distance two. (0,0) to (3,0) is illegal.

5.4 Conversion

After placement, each adjacent enemy-owned cell changes to the moving player's ownership unless protected by its owner's active shield. All applicable neighbors resolve from the destination once.

Newly converted pieces do not trigger additional conversions. Conversion is not a spreading flood fill.

If c enemies convert:

  • Clone: mover gains 1+c pieces; opponent loses c; territory differential improves by 1+2c.
  • Jump: mover gains c pieces; opponent loses c; territory differential improves by 2c.

Example: before a move the scores are 12–12. A clone converting three opponents produces 16–9. A jump converting three instead produces 15–9. The attractive immediate gain must still be weighed against the position left behind.

Only cloning increases total occupancy. Starting with six pieces, a full board requires exactly 52 successful clones in total, irrespective of how many jumps or conversions occur. Therefore the absence of a turn cap would permit long jump cycles.

5.5 Turn resolution order

The current rules resolve a normal move as follows:

  1. Validate the source and destination.
  2. If jumping, clear the source and any shield attached to that source.
  3. Occupy the destination; award its resource capture bonus if applicable.
  4. Convert unprotected adjacent enemy cells and award bonuses for converted resource nodes.
  5. Record the move with player, coordinates, kind, converted cells and round.
  6. Expire shields belonging to the opponent, whose one opposing turn has now completed.
  7. Increment the individual-turn count, switch active player, and clear the per-turn shield-purchase flag.
  8. Record the new board-cycle key and check termination. If terminal, settle the result now; capture/conversion bonuses remain earned, but no recurring income or round advancement follows.
  9. If still active and Player 1 just acted, advance the round label and award income to both players from the resulting board.
  10. If the next player has no legal move and the match is still active, apply an automatic pass.

The board renderer must display the resolved state, not independently recalculate conversions or income.

6. Resource mode

6.1 Public resource ledger

Each player has nonnegative integer balances:

resources[player] = { energy, ore, food }

The starting value of every balance is zero. Resource balances are accumulated, not the current number of controlled nodes. A player can retain banked food after losing all food sites.

Resource-site control is the owner of its occupying piece. An empty node is neutral. Jumping away relinquishes its income immediately; it does not preserve a separate territorial claim.

6.2 Capture awards

When a neutral or enemy node becomes owned by the active player, add one unit of that site's type. This includes:

  • Moving onto an empty node by cloning.
  • Moving onto an empty node by jumping.
  • Converting an adjacent enemy piece on a node.

Plain land gives no resource award. Each newly acquired node is credited once for that acquisition.

Reclaiming a node after it became empty or changed owner can award another capture unit. The game intentionally has no first-capture-only marker. Do not describe resource capture rewards as globally one-time.

6.3 Round income

A full round is one Player 0 turn followed by one Player 1 turn, including any automatic passes. If the state remains active after Player 1 completes that pair, every occupied node adds one unit of its resource to its current owner. Terminal detection happens first; an ended match receives no final recurring payout even when its last action completes a pair.

For player p and type t:

income(p,t) = count of currently p-owned nodes of type t
bank'(p,t) = bank(p,t) + income(p,t)

Both ledgers are credited from the same resolved, nonterminal board. Capturing a site and receiving its round income can both occur in one continuing round.

Example: Player 1 clones onto an empty ore node immediately before round end, and converts an enemy food node. Player 1 receives one ore and one food from the acquisitions. If the game continues and those sites remain owned at payout, they provide another ore and food. If that move ends the game, only the acquisition bonuses apply. There is no extra “double capture”; these are different events.

The current round counter starts at one and advances only after a nonterminal pair. At the 180-turn cap there are at most 89 income payouts; the final round label is 90 because turn 180 terminates before its payout/advancement. Results should explain individual turns and actual totals clearly.

6.4 What each resource does

Ore can be spent on a shield and also contributes to a resource tiebreak if unspent.

Energy contributes only to the resource tiebreak.

Food contributes only to the resource tiebreak.

There is no passive resource multiplier, upkeep, debt, trading, conversion rate, technology purchase or country-dependent production. These absences keep the rules explainable and match the implemented feature set.

6.5 Shield: the only resource ability

A player may spend three ore to protect one currently owned, currently unshielded tile. At most one shield purchase is allowed per turn. This is preparation before the normal move, not an extra movement action.

A purchased shield:

  • Prevents adjacent conversion of that tile.
  • Remains through the owner's move and the opponent's next resolved turn.
  • Expires after that opponent turn, including an automatic pass.
  • Is anchored to the tile; it is removed if the owner jumps away.
  • Does not move with the piece, convert other pieces, create a marker or change jump distance.

The present applyShield operation deducts ore immediately. Selecting an owned tile and pressing the shield control is the purchase; ordinary selection alone costs nothing. The rules have no refund operation, but the current frontend keeps a pre-purchase snapshot for its same-session Undo action. That snapshot is not persisted, so a refund/undo after reloading the page must not be promised.

Three ore earned from the move cannot pay for preparation retroactively. A player with no move is automatically passed; the UI must not offer shield-only turns.

Example: Brazil protects an ore tile and clones elsewhere. Japan then moves beside the protected tile. Other adjacent Brazilian pieces convert normally; the protected tile does not. After Japan's turn its protection expires. If Japan was forced to pass, it still expires.

6.6 Balance mathematics

Node income ceiling. Combined round income cannot exceed the number of resource nodes currently occupied. It is at most 18 units on Frontier and 24 on Struggle.

Type ceilings. There are six ore nodes on Frontier and eight on Struggle, with the same counts for energy and food. If a symmetric split gives each player half of every type, Frontier gives each player three ore, three energy and three food per round; Struggle gives four of each.

Shield sustainability. Ignoring capture bonuses:

  • One held ore node supports a shield every three rounds.
  • Three held ore nodes support one shield per round.
  • Four held ore nodes permit one shield per round and one surplus ore.

The purchase cap remains one per turn even when a player has a large balance. The current economy supports defensive decisions without allowing multiple protected tiles to be purchased on one turn.

Opportunity cost. Spending three ore gives up three units in a possible final resource tiebreak. It cannot compensate for a final territory deficit, because territory is always compared first.

Finite upper bounds. At most 90 pairs occur in 180 individual turns, but the terminal-before-income rule allows at most 89 recurring payouts. A move can acquire at most a destination plus six adjacent resource sites, so seven resource units is a safe per-move capture upper bound, not a claim that the current layouts realize that maximum.

Thus a loose combined pre-spending ledger bound is:

Frontier: 89 × 18 + 180 × 7 = 2,862
Struggle: 89 × 24 + 180 × 7 = 3,396

Automatic passes award no capture bonus. Actual totals will be lower, and spent ore reduces them further. Ordinary integer arithmetic is ample.

Balance questions to measure. First-seat win rate, average match length, ore ownership concentration, shield use, and the frequency of resource ties need actual playtests. Geometric symmetry and these bounds are design evidence, not empirical balance certification.

7. Classic mode

Classic uses the same 58 cells, starting corners and clone/jump/conversion rules. Every tile is plain. Resource gains and shield purchases are disabled; resource totals cannot decide a Classic tie.

Classic is the clean first tutorial and an equally complete way to play. It must not feel like a restricted demo with unavailable purchases or empty upgrade slots.

8. Finite play, passes and results

8.1 Automatic passes

A player may not voluntarily pass while a legal move exists. When the active player has no move, the engine records a forced pass, changes turns and applies the ordinary turn-boundary effects.

One blocked player does not necessarily end the match; the other can continue. If neither side can move, play ends. The engine also tracks consecutive passes as a terminal safeguard.

Passes count toward the 180-turn limit and can complete a round for income if the resulting state remains active. A terminal pass produces no final recurring payout. Passes also consume a shield's one opposing turn. UI feedback should state why a player was passed rather than appearing to skip input silently.

8.2 Terminal conditions

The engine recognizes:

  • Elimination: one side has zero pieces.
  • Full board: no empty playable cell remains.
  • No moves for either side, or two consecutive forced passes.
  • Threefold recurrence of the defined board-cycle key.
  • 180 individual turns.

The current code checks elimination and full board first, then consecutive passes, the turn cap, repetition and mutual immobility. Multiple conditions can be true simultaneously; the recorded reason follows that order, while the score comparison remains the same.

“Turn” means one player's move or pass. A round is a pair of turns. Shield preparation is not a second turn.

8.3 Board-cycle repetition

The implemented key contains:

  • Active player.
  • The ordered ownership of all 58 cells.
  • The presence of active shields.

It deliberately excludes resource balances, absolute round and turn numbers, move-log text and visual selection. The map and mode are fixed within a match.

When that key has occurred three times, the match ends and uses the current score. This is board-cycle repetition, not exact-state repetition: the same board can recur with different banked resources and still trigger the limit.

This definition is especially useful in Resource mode, where growing ledgers could otherwise prevent an exact full-state key from repeating. It also means a player cannot indefinitely farm an unchanged jump cycle for income. The 180-turn cap guarantees a finite upper limit independently.

8.4 Score comparison

At an ordinary terminal result:

  1. Count each player's actually owned cells.
  2. Greater territory wins.
  3. If tied in Resource mode, compare remaining energy + ore + food.
  4. If still tied, declare a draw.
  5. In Classic, a territorial tie is a draw.

The display may show resources beside territory, but must not add them into a single weighted “total score.” The engine's primary total is territory; the resource total is a tiebreak.

Examples:

  • 30 tiles and zero resources beats 28 tiles and 200 resources.
  • 29–29 with resource totals 14–12 is a Resource win for the first player.
  • 29–29 in Classic is a draw.
  • Elimination displays actual counts and surviving ownership. Do not fill empty cells or award fictitious resource captures.

8.5 Surrender, restart and result explanation

The current frontend provides restart and concession confirmation. On concession it cancels the AI callback, sets the winner explicitly, persists the terminal state and displays results. The rules module's terminal-reason union does not model a surrender reason, so an explicit concession reason in the results/log remains a presentation improvement. A future change must preserve the current terminal state rather than letting Continue reopen a live match.

A result panel should contain the winning team or draw, final territory, resource balances when relevant, end reason, turns played and the next action. Do not describe a turn-limit result as elimination or an AI resignation.

9. Countries and fair representation

The present registry contains Brazil, Japan, Canada, Norway, Kenya and Australia. These are selectable team identities with hand-authored small flag illustrations and presentation colors.

Rules receive Player 0 or Player 1, not a country-specific ability table. AI difficulty is a separate selection. A country swap with the same seat, mode, map and move history must yield identical legal moves and outcomes.

Guidelines for adding another country:

  • Use a consistent contemporary common name and an accurate, legible simplified flag.
  • Avoid territorial maps, disputed borders, political slogans and military emblems.
  • Give no economic, social or intelligence stereotype a gameplay effect.
  • Use the same card layout, typographic weight and selection treatment.
  • Keep a text name beside the flag so recognition never depends on a symbol.
  • Use seat-specific outlines or markers when palette choices are too similar.

The chosen team colors do not define the resource colors. Ore, energy and food retain their own icon shapes and labels across every matchup.

Local hotseat has public information. A privacy handoff screen is optional polish rather than a secret-information requirement; neither player has hidden cards or orders.

10. Computer opponents

10.1 Shared constraints

The AI uses the same state and move generator as a human. It receives no extra pieces, resources, capture range, vision or illegal shield. There is no remote model call and no downloaded policy. “AI” here means deterministic local game-tree and heuristic logic.

The implementation returns null only when no move is available. Automatic passes and end rules belong to the engine; the UI must not spin an AI timer forever on null.

10.2 Cadet: varied short-term decisions

Cadet ranks the same immediate legal moves, considers up to the best eight, then selects by a deterministic index:

index = (moveCount × 7 + round × 3) mod candidateCount

It therefore varies without a runtime random source, is reproducible from a saved state, and can miss the top immediate move. It is not a random-move bot and does not deliberately break rules.

10.3 Commander: best immediate evaluation

Commander picks the top-ranked legal move. Its ordering value is:

2 × immediateConversions
+ 1 if clone, otherwise 0
+ 0.65 if the destination is a resource node in Resource mode
+ 0.035 × adjacentFriendlyCount
− 0.015 × destinationRadius

Stable coordinate ordering breaks equal values. Commander currently does not search opponent replies. Copy must not promise multi-turn planning at this difficulty.

10.4 Strategist: bounded reply search

Strategist considers the ten highest-ranked moves and up to six ranked replies to each. It minimizes over those replies and chooses the move with the strongest resulting evaluation. It is a beam-limited two-ply search, not exhaustive minimax, a proven solution or an adaptive learning opponent.

The evaluation from a player's perspective is:

terminal win: +100,000
terminal loss: −100,000
terminal draw: 0

otherwise:
  10 × territoryDifference
  + 0.3 × reachableEmptyDestinationDifference
  + 1.8 × controlledNodeDifference, in Resource mode
  + 0.03 × bankedResourceDifference, in Resource mode

A forced pass can return the move to the same player; the search handles that resolved engine state rather than assuming strict alternation after every submitted move.

The exact coefficients are inspectable implementation facts, not a promise of strength. Future tuning should preserve territory as the terminal objective and keep difficulty deterministic for bug reproduction.

10.5 Shield selection

The separate chooseShield helper evaluates owned, affordable, unshielded tiles with a plausible enemy landing beside them. It scores threatened landings, nearby friendly pieces and a modest resource-site preference, with a stronger weight on ore.

This is a preparation heuristic. The inspected frontend chooses a normal move first, passes it to chooseShield, applies an affordable chosen shield, then submits that move. The helper avoids protecting a source that the planned jump will vacate. The move search does not exhaustively search every shield-plus-move combination or jointly optimize the ore expenditure. This is source-level integration evidence, not an observed browser shield event.

10.6 Scheduling and cancellation

The frontend should make one AI action for one current state, show a short thinking indicator, and reject stale callbacks after restart, new setup, home navigation or save restoration. Resource preparation and the following move must refer to the same active seat.

A fixed pause can improve readability, but must not be advertised as processing time or used to hide a nonresponsive browser. Actual slow-device measurements are still required. A worker or deeper search is a backlog option only if measured search blocks input.

11. Screen concepts and information hierarchy

11.1 Home: the quiet sea

Use the original archipelago image full bleed, with a dark ocean area behind live title text. A small bronze eyebrow introduces the setting; the large ivory title is the main anchor.

Primary action: New skirmish.

Secondary actions: Continue when a valid save exists, How to play, and sound/settings.

Show a concise promise near the primary action: “A shared world. A different frontier every move.” Avoid claiming procedural maps; the “different” refers to player decisions. The subtitle may instead be strictly descriptive: “Clone, leap and connect across 58 hexes.”

The hero image contains no baked title. All typography and buttons remain real interface elements.

11.2 Setup: the expedition desk

The setup must expose, without an advanced-options drawer:

  • Solo or local hotseat.
  • Player country and opponent country.
  • Cadet, Commander or Strategist for solo.
  • Resource or Classic.
  • Frontier or Struggle, with a precise note that Classic removes deposits.
  • Start and back actions.

Both country cards use equal visual prominence. Each explains that countries have identical stats. Default choices should form a readable color contrast, not a claim that one country is better.

Each map card uses a small schematic plus its concrete rule difference: 18 versus 24 resource nodes. Never promise different board sizes.

11.3 Match: the survey table

Recommended desktop hierarchy:

Top bar: brand · mode/map · round/turn · sound/settings · home
Left ledger: both teams, territory, resources, active-player indicator
Center: largest possible interactive Phaser board
Right/context area: selection, clone/jump help, shield and recent moves
Bottom or side actions: keyboard board, restart and surrender

The active player must be identifiable by text and shape as well as color. Territory is the largest number in each team ledger. Resource values are secondary with distinct icons.

Selecting a piece reveals legal clone and jump destinations. The selected coordinate and instructions update immediately. Clicking another owned piece changes selection. Clicking an illegal destination leaves the match state unchanged and provides short, nonblocking feedback.

A shield control states both price and effect: “Shield · 3 ore · blocks conversion for one opponent turn.” If unaffordable, show the reason in text rather than a silent disabled icon.

11.4 Contextual help

Help content follows the player's action:

  • Nothing selected: “Choose one of your markers.”
  • Piece selected: “Solid targets clone. Ring targets jump.”
  • Resource node inspected: explain capture bonus, round income and current owner.
  • Shield targeting: explain immediate purchase and the need to move afterward.
  • AI turn: explain the current opponent and difficulty; prevent human actions.
  • Forced pass: state that no legal move was available.
  • Result: explain the exact terminal reason and comparison.

The always-available rules summary covers conversion, scoring, the three-ore shield and board-cycle repetition. No tooltip may advertise an unimplemented resource ability.

11.5 Move log

Each human-readable record should identify:

  • Acting country/seat.
  • Clone, jump or pass.
  • Source and destination where applicable.
  • Number of conversions.
  • Round or turn context.

The engine stores converted coordinate IDs, which can support a richer replay later. The present frontend displays the three most recent move summaries; source/destination are available in the engine records but are not all printed in that compact log. A move log is currently an explanation aid, not a promised replay feature. Shield purchase history is not in MoveRecord; a future detailed audit log must add an explicit event instead of guessing it from the final balance.

The frontend also provides Undo round in solo and Undo turn in hotseat through an in-memory state stack. It snapshots paid shield preparation separately, so undo can restore the pre-purchase state in the same session. Neither undo history nor that preparation snapshot is persisted. Do not promise an undo history or a preparation refund after browser reload.

11.6 Results and recovery

The result panel uses calm celebratory light, not explosions or a military victory scene. Display both teams and the exact score comparison.

Primary: Play again or rematch.

Secondary: Change setup and home.

Restart and surrender require an explicit outcome for the saved session. A stale local save must not silently undo a finished result. A failed optional audio load must never prevent the result panel.

12. Responsive behavior, input and accessibility

12.1 Layout priorities

Desktop target: a generous board with both ledgers visible at common 1366 × 768 and 1440 × 900 CSS-pixel windows.

Tablet target: collapse secondary help/log panels before shrinking the board excessively. In portrait, place the team strip above the board and controls below it.

Narrow-screen target: retain a usable setup and an alternative cell-control interface. At approximately 390 CSS pixels wide, fitting all 58 visual targets with generous hit areas is difficult; do not claim mobile acceptance from CSS breakpoints alone.

The current Electron window has an 820 × 620 minimum. That is a desktop-shell constraint, not proof of mobile responsiveness or the browser's minimum size.

12.2 Pointer and touch

  • Click/tap to select, then click/tap a destination.
  • No drag, double-click, precision hover or right-click is required.
  • Hit testing must match the visible hex face and reject holes.
  • Use destination markers as well as highlight color.
  • Do not scroll the page unexpectedly when interacting with the board.

The current board scene implements pointer selection and responsive redraw. A reduced-motion option suppresses its conversion rings. Its clone/jump indicators are visibly different circle treatments; dashed paths and trajectory previews are future polish, not current assets.

12.3 Keyboard and assistive technology

Canvas graphics are not sufficient accessibility. The current frontend supplies a Keyboard board toggle and 58 DOM cell buttons that call the same clickCell/rules path as pointer input. Their labels identify coordinate, owner, terrain and legal-destination status, and selection uses aria-pressed. This is source evidence of a real alternative, not a browser keyboard acceptance result. An explicit shield label on those cell controls is still an accessibility improvement.

The acceptance target is a real DOM alternative exposing each cell's coordinate, owner, terrain, shield and availability. Controls should call the same validated move path as pointer input. A keyboard user must be able to choose a source and destination, shield, inspect help, restart, surrender and dismiss results.

Use visible focus, ordinary tab order, Enter/Space activation, Escape to dismiss transient selection, and appropriate dialog focus restoration. Do not capture browser shortcuts. A continuously changing move log should not create overwhelming announcements.

12.4 Perception and motion

  • Names, seat markers and outlines supplement country color.
  • Resource shapes differ: energy bolt, ore crystal and food leaf.
  • Normal text should target at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text and essential nontext controls at least 3:1.
  • Small decorative caption text is not a substitute for essential instructions.
  • Respect a reduced-motion setting and the operating-system preference where the frontend implements it.
  • No rule depends on hearing an effect.
  • No flashing, camera shake or urgent audio countdown is needed.

These are design and acceptance targets, not a claim of audited WCAG conformance.

14. Audio direction and delivered cues

14.1 Musical identity

The audio should suggest curiosity, sea air and a well-made physical instrument. Use soft wooden plucks, light glass resonances, warm low pads and restrained stereo texture. Avoid orchestral war music, national anthems, recognizable borrowed tunes and realistic battle sound.

The delivered composition is an original 24-second, eight-bar loop in 4/4 at 80 beats per minute. Its source describes a D Dorian miniature with a lower-register answer in the second half. Circularly written tails and echoes support the seam; it is not a commercially licensed recording.

14.2 Delivered sound files

All current WAVs are stereo, 44,100 Hz, 16-bit PCM:

  • archipelago-loop.wav — 24.00 seconds; optional atmosphere.
  • select.wav — 0.18 seconds; a focused marker or deliberate menu selection.
  • move.wav — 0.42 seconds; a confirmed legal clone or jump.
  • capture.wav — 0.67 seconds; one conversion event cluster.
  • resource.wav — 0.65 seconds; resource acquisition/income feedback.
  • victory.wav — 2.60 seconds; one result reveal.
  • invalid.wav — 0.26 seconds; soft unavailable-action feedback.

Do not play one full capture sound for every converted tile at the same instant. A single action-level cue can acknowledge the cluster and prevent clipping or fatigue. Resource sound should likewise summarize a payout rather than firing once per banked unit.

14.3 Runtime contract

AudioManager loads local files using Vite's base URL. Unlock must be triggered by a user gesture. Music is opt-in; it does not start at class construction. Muting and volume change a shared gain. Missing or denied audio is optional and must not block gameplay.

The source supports play, startMusic, stopMusic, setMuted, setVolume and destroy. The frontend owns settings persistence and screen-to-screen lifetime. Leaving a match must not leak multiple looping sources.

14.4 Audio backlog

  • A distinct quiet shield-purchase cue, if the existing resource cue proves ambiguous.
  • A noncelebratory draw/concede response, avoiding a triumphant cue for every terminal state.
  • A longer optional menu arrangement, with a small asset budget.
  • Listening acceptance on browser and desktop output devices.
  • Alternative compressed exports only after measured download needs justify them.

No voice narration, procedural music service or runtime generative audio is needed. Header/peak inspection is file evidence; it is not a listening acceptance claim.

15. Save and continuation design

15.1 Persistence boundaries

The browser's local storage is a local convenience. It can be cleared, denied, tied to a particular origin, or isolated by an embedded frame/browser policy. It is not an account, backup service, cloud promise or tamper-proof competitive record.

The optional desktop shell uses a different browser storage context and may not share a web save. The frontend can download a JSON field record, but an import flow and a verified browser-to-desktop round trip remain backlog. Export alone is not seamless continuation.

15.2 Required logical payload

A recoverable match needs:

  • Format version; a save timestamp is useful future metadata but is not in the current payload.
  • Mode, map, solo/hotseat, countries and difficulty.
  • The complete resolved GameState.
  • Relevant presentation preferences, separately versioned if convenient.

The actual frontend persists the following logical TypeScript contract under the localStorage key hexxago-frontier-v1:

interface SavedExpeditionV1 {
  version: 1;
  state: GameState;
  config: {
    factions: [string, string];
    opponent: 'solo' | 'hotseat';
    difficulty: 'cadet' | 'commander' | 'strategist';
    mode: 'resource' | 'classic';
    map: 'frontier' | 'struggle';
  };
  prefs: {
    muted: boolean;
    music: boolean;
    volume: number;
    reducedMotion: boolean;
  };
}

GameState is the complete serializable model in section 16, including all 58 Tile objects, resource ledgers, move and position histories, current player/round, moveCount, passes, winner and shieldUsedThisTurn. It optionally includes lastMove and endReason. The same envelope is used for the downloadable hexxago-expedition.json field record.

Defaults are Brazil/Japan, solo Commander, Resource/Frontier, sound unmuted, music off and volume 0.45. The initial reduced-motion preference reads the operating-system media query. No savedAt field or import API is currently serialized.

15.3 What must survive restoration

Preserve active player, every owner, banks, active shields, shieldUsedThisTurn, pass count, individual-turn count and repetition history. Reconstructing only the visible board can change a future terminal result or allow a second shield purchase in the same turn.

The log is valuable for explanation. The position history is required for the implemented repetition rule. AI timers, animation objects, DOM references, audio buffers and Phaser objects must not be serialized.

15.4 Validation and failure UX

The current restoration code checks version one, a 58-element tiles array, history/resources/positionHistory arrays, a legal current player, a recognized state mode, recognized faction IDs and solo/hotseat. Parsing/storage exceptions fall back to a new match.

This is partial structural validation. Stronger acceptance should also verify expected map/difficulty enums, exactly two faction IDs, legal owner values, 58 unique playable IDs, coordinate agreement, finite nonnegative integer banks, a bounded move count and a sensible result state.

Handle JSON parsing and storage access errors without discarding a currently valid match silently. Offer a clear start-new action for an invalid or unsupported save. Do not silently call an old save “migrated” unless a concrete migration has run.

Save after committed state changes. If shield preparation can be saved before movement, it must preserve both the deduction and purchase flag. Result/surrender state must be persisted consistently or the unfinished save intentionally cleared.

The frontend currently catches storage-write failures without blocking play; it does not display a storage-denied indicator. Stronger semantic validation, unsupported-save messaging and visible persistence failure feedback remain acceptance improvements rather than delivered guarantees.

  • src/game/ai.ts — deterministic move ranking, difficulty selection and shield heuristic.
  • src/game/BoardScene.ts — Phaser drawing, hit testing, selection overlays and conversion feedback.
  • src/main.ts — DOM application shell, setup/help/settings/results dialogs, move orchestration, local save, keyboard cell controls, undo and AI scheduling.

The frontend appeared during this documentation pass and was read back for this final source description. That source presence does not establish its browser interaction acceptance. This architecture does not require an additional frontend framework or a rewritten engine.

16.3 State flow

Keep one authoritative GameState in the orchestration layer:

input intent
   → selection/setup validation
   → rules applyShield/applyMove/applyPass
   → one new resolved state
   → update DOM ledger, help, log and accessible controls
   → BoardScene.setView
   → local save
   → optional action-level sound
   → schedule next AI action only if still appropriate

The board scene reports cell IDs. It does not mutate ownership. The AI returns a move or shield choice. It does not own the rendering lifecycle. Audio failures do not roll back or repeat an already applied move.

16.4 Serializable engine model

Important fields:

  • Tile: id, q, r, terrain, owner, optional shieldedBy.
  • Move: from and to.
  • MoveRecord: player, source/destination or null, clone/jump/pass, converted IDs, round.
  • GameState: tiles, turn, round, two resource ledgers, history, winner, passes, mode, map, moveCount, positionHistory, shieldUsedThisTurn, optional lastMove/endReason.

The rules copy tiles, banks and history arrays before changing them. Consumers must treat returned states as the current snapshot and avoid mutating the engine's previous state for convenience.

16.5 Rendering and asset loading

The scene uses Phaser Graphics for board geometry and local text objects. Its resize handler recomputes positions to fit the available canvas. The hero image is a menu/background asset, not a tile hit-test map.

All shipped assets must be local and use relative/base-aware paths. No CDN font, remote sprite service or analytics call should be necessary for a match. A browser reload still needs a host or an explicitly implemented offline cache; offline-capable game logic alone does not create a service worker.

18. Acceptance cases for implementation review

These are concrete checks for the integration owner to execute as authorized, not a fabricated list of completed tests.

Geometry and movement

  • Count exactly 58 cells and six starting pieces.
  • Verify every resource label and opening owner under S(q,r)=(r,q).
  • Reject hole destinations, occupied targets, enemy sources and distance-three moves.
  • Accept a distance-two jump even when the intervening location is a hole.
  • Confirm clone retains the source; jump clears it and its shield.
  • Resolve several adjacent conversions together and stop after one adjacency step.

Resource ledger

  • Award a destination resource exactly once.
  • Award every newly converted node exactly once.
  • Award both sides' round income once after Player 1's resolved nonterminal turn, including a nonterminal pass.
  • Award capture/conversion bonuses but no recurring income on a terminal action, whether Player 0 or Player 1 acted.
  • Do not award income after Player 0's turn alone.
  • Allow a recaptured/reoccupied node to receive a new capture bonus.
  • Keep Energy/Food as tiebreak values, without an undocumented ability.
  • Verify Classic has plain terrain, no shield purchase and no resource tiebreak.

Shield

  • Reject an enemy, empty or already shielded target and insufficient ore.
  • Deduct exactly three ore once; preserve the movement action.
  • Reject a second purchase during the same turn.
  • Prevent only that tile's conversion through one opposing move or pass.
  • Remove protection after the opposing turn.
  • Remove it if its owner jumps away.
  • Preserve paid preparation through Continue; do not allow a second purchase after reload.

Ends and loops

  • Eliminate a side without inventing an automatic board fill.
  • Show one forced pass and continue when the other side has moves.
  • End mutual immobility.
  • End on the third board-cycle key even when ledgers differ.
  • End at 180 individual turns.
  • Compare territory before resources and permit an exact draw.
  • Persist or clear surrender/results consistently so Continue cannot resurrect an active game.

AI and integration

  • Produce the same AI move for the same state and difficulty.
  • Use legal engine moves and ordinary resource costs.
  • Handle forced passes and terminal moves without scheduling another invalid action.
  • Cancel stale AI actions after restart/home/new setup.
  • Keep source state unmodified when applying a move.
  • Keep both human seats operable in hotseat.

Presentation and storage

  • Complete a pointer match and a keyboard-driven sequence using the same rule path.
  • Read owner, terrain and shield without relying on color alone.
  • Resize without hidden controls or mismatched hit targets.
  • Disable sound, toggle music, and continue playing if audio fails.
  • Resume a valid save with all counters/shields/repetition state intact.
  • Reject malformed or unsupported saves with useful feedback.
  • Load the production build from a subdirectory and the desktop local bundle.

19. Principal risks and deliberately deferred work

Source and delivery drift. Parallel changes can leave rules, help and packaging out of agreement. Reconcile module interfaces and update status from actual artifacts; separate component completion does not establish application integration.

Economy ambiguity. Capture plus income, reacquisition and board-cycle repetition are intentionally explicit. If changed later, update rules, save compatibility, help and AI together.

First/second seat effects. Reflection proves structural access, not empirical fairness. Rematch seat swapping and measured self-play are future improvements, not a reason to invent country advantages.

AI cost and strength. Beam limits bound work, but latency still needs device measurement. Difficulty names are descriptive tiers, not a rating claim.

Canvas accessibility. An ARIA label alone is insufficient. The DOM alternative and real keyboard acceptance are required before advertising complete keyboard support.

Desktop/platform claims. Electron configuration is not OS acceptance, code signing, Steam Cloud, controller support or Steam Deck verification.

Save fragility. Browser origin changes, private mode and quota policy can remove or deny a save. Cloud synchronization and save import are not shipped; local JSON export is available.

Asset provenance. Generated art is original project material under applicable service terms, without an assertion of exclusive copyright. Historical assets and reference branding are excluded.

Deferred: online multiplayer, ranked play, accounts, achievements, cloud saves, save import/verified cross-distribution transfer, campaigns, nation abilities, resource tech trees, procedural maps, gamepad support, map editor, replay viewer, localization beyond the delivered UI, mobile-store applications, and platform certification. None should appear as available in shipped descriptions until implemented and verified.

Resource income, countries, defensive shields and the board art are new design choices.

Design decisions drawn from the research

  1. Preserve the original move vocabulary. Players should learn clone, jump and convert without an economy blocking ordinary movement.
  2. Give resource control a visible spatial role. Ore funds one temporary defensive shield; energy and food accumulate toward the resource tie-break. No unimplemented units or abilities are advertised.
  3. Borrow HEXSTORM’s sense of a crafted miniature world: clear resource silhouettes, warm land against deep water, restrained movement and feedback. HEXXAGO has original assets and a turn-based board rather than HEXSTORM’s tower defence systems.
  4. Use real countries as equal-stat sporting factions on an imagined archipelago. Country identity changes flag, colour and label. It does not encode national stereotypes, military strength or real borders.
  5. Ship one local-first core to browser and desktop. No account, external API, paid currency or runtime generative model is required.

Sound design and reproduction

Run python3 scripts/generate-audio.py to regenerate the complete sound set. Only Python's standard library is required. All randomness has a fixed seed, so the generated WAV bytes are reproducible with the same Python math behavior. There is no network request or outside sample dependency.

The 24-second music loop is eight bars of 4/4 at 80 beats per minute. A sparse original D Dorian phrase sits over low warm sine harmonics. The higher notes use a soft wooden resonator, with glass-like color reserved for resources. Circularly written note tails, crossed stereo echoes, and circular filtered noise preserve the loop boundary. Slow noise envelopes suggest a distant sea without a literal or repetitive wave recording. The second four bars answer the first at a lower register.

Sound meanings:

  • Selection is a brief soft upper wooden tap.
  • Movement is a restrained rising two-note pluck.
  • Conversion is a warmer three-note glass cluster.
  • Resource collection is a brighter rising pair.
  • Victory resolves into a longer major-colored arpeggio and low pad.
  • Invalid input uses a quiet low paired pulse without a harsh error buzzer.

src/audio.ts exports AudioManager. Call unlock() directly from a user gesture to satisfy browser audio policy. Call play(name) for interaction events. Music is opt-in through startMusic() and stops with stopMusic(); the class never starts music during construction. setMuted() and setVolume() control a shared gain, and destroy() stops sources, closes the audio context, and aborts pending fetches. Asset URLs respect Vite's BASE_URL. The frontend owns settings persistence and whether music should continue between screens.
